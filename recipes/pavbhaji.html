Pav Bhaji recipe

Description

Pav Bhaji is a world-famous fast food dish and perhaps the king of street food in the western state of Maharashtra. The recipe is an amalgamation of vegetables spiced with a unique blend of spices known as pav bhaji masala and served with a soft bread roll, aka pav.

Ingridients

For bhaji:

  • 3 to mato, finely chopped
  • ¼ cup peas / matar
  • ½ capsicum, finely chopped
  • 2 potato, boiled & mashed
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp + ¼ tsp kashmiri red chilli powder / lal mirch powder
  • ¼ tsp turmeric / haldi
  • 1 tsp + ½ tsp pav bhaji masala
  • 1 tsp + 1 tsp kasuri methi / dry fenugreek leaves
  • 2 tbsp + 1 tbsp coriander leaves, finely chop ped
  • 1 tsp ginger garlic paste
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • ½ lemon juice
  • 3 drops red food colour, optional
  • water to adjust consistency
  • 1 tbsp + 1 tbsp butter

To toast pav:

  • 8 pav / bread roll
  • 4 tsp butter
  • ½ tsp kashmiri red chilli powder / lal mirch powder
  • ½ tsp pav bhaji masala
  • 4 tsp coriander leaves, finely chopped

Steps

  1. Boil and Mash
    1. Add the potatoes, cauliflower, peas, carrots, and beetroot into a pressure cooker or pot.
    2. Add just enough water to cover them, along with a pinch of salt and turmeric.
    3. Pressure cook for 3–4 whistles (or boil until completely soft).
    4. Drain the excess water (save it for later) and use a potato masher to mash the vegetables into a smooth consistency while they are still hot.
  2. Build the Masala Base
    1. Heat a large flat pan or kadhai over medium heat. Melt 2 tablespoons of butter with 1 teaspoon of oil (this prevents the butter from burning).
    2. Sauté the chopped onions until they turn translucent.
    3. Stir in the ginger-garlic paste and fry for 1 minute until fragrant.
    4. 1. Add the capsicum and cook for 2 minutes, then mix in the tomatoes. Cook down until the tomatoes are completely mushy and start releasing oil at the edges.
  3. Simmer the Bhaji
    1. Turn the heat to low. Add the Pav Bhaji Masala and Kashmiri red chilli powder. Stir well for 30 seconds.
    2. Pour in the mashed vegetables along with the reserved vegetable stock.
    3. Mix thoroughly. If the mixture is too thick, add a splash of hot water. The consistency shoold be pourable but thick.
    4. Let it simmer on low heat for 8–10 minutes so the flavors merge. Taste and adjust the salt.
    5. Finish by stirring in a handfol of fresh chopped coriander, a squeeze of lemon juice, and a final heaping tablespoon of butter. Turn off the heat.
  4. Toast the Pav
    1. Slice the pav rolls horizontally down the middle, keeping them attached at the back.
    2. Melt a tablespoon of butter on a hot tawa or skillet.
    3. Street-style tip: Sprinkle a tiny pinch of pav bhaji masala and fresh coriander directly into the melting butter.
    4. Press the pav open-faced into the seasoned butter. Toast both sides until warm, soft, and slightly golden.
  5. Assemble and Serve
    1. Ladle the steaming hot bhaji into a shallow plate.
    2. Top it with a fresh slab of butter.
    3. Serve immediately next to the toasted pav, accompanied by a side of finely chopped raw onions and a lemon wedge.
